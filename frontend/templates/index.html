Frequently Asked Questions
There's no such thing as a universal ATS score — despite what every CV-writing influencer on LinkedIn would have you believe. Applicant Tracking Systems vary wildly: some count keywords, some parse structured data (job titles, dates, education), some use machine learning models trained on their own hiring outcomes, and some don't score at all — they just store and organise applications, leaving ranking entirely to the recruiter. Anyone claiming to give you "your ATS score" is either misinformed or selling something. What you can meaningfully measure is how well your CV's content maps to a specific job description — which is what this tool does.
It measures how closely the meaning of your CV matches the requirements of the job description — regardless of exact wording. It uses a local AI model (FastEmbed) to compare the conceptual content of your CV against each requirement in the JD. This means it catches matches that keyword counting would miss: "owned data pipeline infrastructure" will score as strongly similar to "platform infrastructure ownership" even though the words are different. It runs entirely on your machine — no API call, no cost, same result every time.
They answer different questions. The Semantic Score is consistent and free — same inputs, same result every time. It's good at identifying whether your experience conceptually maps to what the JD is asking for. The AI Assessment (powered by Claude) is interpretive — it understands context and nuance that a similarity score can't capture. It can reason about why a gap is structural versus addressable, and give you a plain-English view of how a recruiter might read your application. Where they agree, confidence is high. Where they diverge, that's worth investigating.
The difference between your Summary score and your Professional Experience score. A summary can claim almost anything — "proven track record", "experienced platform PM", "data-driven leader" — at zero cost. The Professional Experience section has to prove those claims with real roles, real companies, and ideally real metrics. A high Summary score paired with a low Experience score means your CV is making promises the evidence doesn't keep. That's a yellow flag for any experienced recruiter, even if the ATS doesn't catch it.
Structural — the skill or experience genuinely doesn't exist in your background. No amount of reframing will close this gap. It's worth knowing early so you can decide whether the application is worth pursuing.

Domain transfer — you have the underlying skill, but not in the specific domain the JD requires. Often addressable with targeted reframing: the experience is real, it just needs to be presented through the right lens.

Keyword gap — the experience almost certainly exists in your background, but your CV uses different vocabulary to describe it. Addressable with targeted rewording.

Seniority gap — the skill is present but at a different level than the role requires. Partially addressable depending on the size of the gap.
The Semantic Score runs entirely locally — your CV text never leaves your machine for that step. The AI Assessment and CV reorder features send your CV and JD text to the Anthropic Claude API for processing. Anthropic's data handling policies apply to that data. The job tracker stores your application data locally in a SQLite database on your machine. Nothing is stored on any external server by this tool.
